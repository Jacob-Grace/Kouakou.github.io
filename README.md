This project investigates disparities in chronic disease prevalence across different demographic and geographic populations in the United States. The goal is to explore how factors such as race, income, education, and location relate to chronic disease outcomes using public datasets.

---

In many underserved communities, chronic illnesses such as diabetes, asthma, and hypertension continue to rise due to unequal access to healthcare, environmental exposures, and systemic inequities. This project uses data science techniques to describe, visualize, and interpret these disparities.

The analysis focuses on:
- Cleaning and transforming publicly available datasets (e.g., CDC, census data)
- Descriptive statistics and visual summaries of chronic disease prevalence
- Comparing regional trends across different population subgroups
- Highlighting gaps in care and resource allocation

---

The insights generated are intended to inform discussions around health equity and support future research and public health planning.
